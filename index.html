01Machine Learning

Telecom Customer Churn Prediction

Developed and evaluated machine learning models to predict customer churn using a telecommunications dataset containing 7,043 customer records. Compared Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost to identify churn patterns and assess how predictive analytics can support customer retention strategies.

Dataset

Telco Customer Churn dataset (7,043 records, 21 original columns; 1,409 customers in the test set).

Approach

Data cleaning, feature engineering, encoding, scaling, stratified 80/20 split, training-only SMOTE, and evaluation of three classifiers.

Tools

Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, seaborn, scikit-learn, imbalanced-learn, and XGBoost.

Key findings / outcome

Logistic Regression achieved ROC-AUC 0.8415, churn recall 75.40%, and churn F1 0.6211. Random Forest achieved the highest overall accuracy (76.37%). These results demonstrate the trade-off between identifying churners and overall classification accuracy.

Limitations

Some preprocessing was performed before the train/test split, creating possible information leakage. Results are illustrative and require independent validation before operational use.

Skills demonstrated

Classification, feature engineering, imbalanced-data handling, model evaluation, and business-focused interpretation.

02Data Visualization

U.S. Childcare Affordability Analysis

Analyzed U.S. childcare pricing data from 2008 to 2018 to examine affordability trends across geographic locations, provider types, and child age groups. Used Python-based exploratory data analysis and visualizations to identify cost differences and provide insights that support informed discussions about childcare affordability.

Dataset

National Database of Childcare Prices (34,567 original records; 23,342 cleaned records and 17 selected columns).

Approach

Exploratory data analysis, cleaning, geographic and provider-type comparisons, income-versus-price visualization, and six saved figures.

Tools

Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, seaborn, and OpenPyXL.

Key findings / outcome

The annualized average center-based infant childcare price was $15,786 in the District of Columbia versus $4,088 in Mississippi. Center-based infant care averaged $7,576 annually, compared with $5,896 for home-based care in the analyzed records.

Limitations

Annualized figures are weekly recorded prices multiplied by 52, not actual household spending. State averages are not population-weighted, and observed income-price relationships do not establish causation.

Skills demonstrated

Data cleaning, exploratory analysis, geographic comparisons, data visualization, and infographic communication.

03Generative AI

AI-Based Residential Energy Efficiency Optimization

Developed a generative AI advisor using 19,735 household energy and environmental records to produce structured residential energy-efficiency guidance. Fine-tuned GPT-2 with LoRA using 510 generated prompt-response examples and evaluated response quality, formatting, and hallucination risks.

Dataset

Appliances Energy Prediction dataset: 19,735 source records; five selected energy and environmental measurements; 510 balanced prompt-response examples (459 training and 51 validation).

Approach

Categorized sensor readings, generated structured Assessment, Recommendations, and Limitations examples, compared baseline GPT-2 with LoRA fine-tuning across five scenarios, tested four hallucination-risk prompts, and saved and reloaded the adapter.

Tools

Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, PEFT / LoRA, and GPT-2.

Key findings / outcome

Completed three fine-tuning epochs and 171 training steps, with final validation loss 0.216380. The saved adapter reloaded successfully and produced the required section headings. These metrics do not establish recommendation accuracy or energy savings.

Limitations

Generated recommendations were not independently validated for energy or cost savings. Reloaded output included repetition and inconsistent numbering; expert review and real-world testing are needed.

Skills demonstrated

Prompt engineering, data preparation, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, qualitative model evaluation, hallucination-risk testing, and model persistence.

05Forecasting

U.S. Retail Sales Forecasting Using Time-Series Analysis

Developed and evaluated a SARIMA time-series forecasting model using 354 monthly U.S. retail sales observations from January 1992 through June 2021. Analyzed historical trends and seasonality, forecast the final 12 months, and assessed prediction errors to illustrate challenges in forecasting during changing economic conditions.

Dataset

U.S. retail sales CSV: 30 annual rows reshaped into 354 monthly observations (January 1992–June 2021). Training: 342 months; testing: 12 months.

Approach

Reshaped year-by-month data, explored trends and seasonality, applied a chronological train/test split, fitted SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,1,1,12), and compared 12-month forecasts with actual sales.

Tools

Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, and statsmodels (SARIMAX).

Key findings / outcome

Test RMSE was 59,819.07 in the dataset’s reported sales units. Forecasts underestimated actual sales in all 12 test months; the largest shortfall occurred in April 2021 (approximately 125,652 units).

Limitations

One SARIMA specification was tested on a single 12-month holdout period. No seasonal-naïve benchmark or rolling-origin validation was reported; rapid changes in 2021 were not captured.

Skills demonstrated

Time-series reshaping, seasonal forecasting, chronological validation, error analysis, and communicating model limitations.

06Recommender Systems

Hybrid Movie Recommendation System Using MovieLens

Developed a hybrid movie recommendation system using the MovieLens Small dataset, combining user-rating patterns with movie-tag similarities. Applied collaborative filtering, TF-IDF, cosine similarity, and fuzzy title matching to generate ten ranked movie recommendations for a user-selected title.

Dataset

MovieLens Small movies.csv, ratings.csv, and tags.csv; constructed a user–movie ratings matrix of 610 users by 9,724 rated movies.

Approach

Built item-based collaborative cosine similarities from ratings and TF-IDF cosine similarities from aggregated movie tags; combined them with 70% collaborative and 30% tag-based weights, plus fuzzy title matching.

Tools

Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, TF-IDF, cosine similarity, fuzzywuzzy, python-Levenshtein, and tabulate.

Key findings / outcome

The interactive prototype returns ten ranked recommendations with release years and average ratings. For Toy Story, recommendations included A Bug’s Life and Toy Story 2; approximate title matching and the exit command were tested.

Limitations

The 70/30 weighting was not optimized, and no held-out recommendation metrics such as precision@K or recall@K were reported. Sparse ratings, missing tags, and ambiguous fuzzy matches can affect results.

Skills demonstrated

Collaborative filtering, content-based recommendation, TF-IDF, similarity modeling, fuzzy matching, and interactive Python workflows.

07Operational Analytics

TSA Passenger Complaint & Airport Operations Analysis

Analyzed TSA passenger complaint records from 2015 through January 2024 to examine complaint trends, leading categories, and differences across major airports. Combined multiple complaint datasets with airport reference information to create six visualizations that support operational monitoring and further investigation.

Dataset

Four CSV files: complaints by airport, category, and subcategory, plus IATA/ICAO airport reference data. Complaint coverage spans 2015–January 2024.

Approach

Cleaned complaint dates and airport codes, visualized monthly and annual trends, compared leading categories and airports, mapped matched locations, and examined reference-data geographic coverage.

Tools

Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas, Matplotlib, and seaborn.

Key findings / outcome

Airport-level complaints fell from 112,610 in 2019 to 45,326 in 2020, then reached 172,729 in 2023. Expedited Passenger Screening Program represented approximately 55.8% of category-level complaints; LAX, JFK, and EWR had among the highest raw airport totals.

Limitations

Counts are not adjusted for passenger volume and do not measure airport service quality. Only January 2024 is available; category and subcategory totals must not be added together, and maps omit unmatched airports.

Skills demonstrated

Operational data analysis, multi-file cleaning, trend analysis, category comparisons, geographic visualization, and responsible interpretation of raw counts.

08Machine Learning

Road Accident Severity Prediction Using Machine Learning

Developed and evaluated machine learning models using 12,316 road accident records from Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, to classify accident severity and examine associated risk factors. Compared Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost to assess predictive performance and provide insights relevant to road safety research.

Dataset

Road Traffic Accident (RTA) dataset: 12,316 records, 32 original columns, three severity classes.

Approach

Exploratory analysis, chi-square and Cramér’s V association tests, feature engineering, stratified splitting, and five-fold cross-validation across eight tuned configurations.

Tools

Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, seaborn, SciPy, scikit-learn, and XGBoost.

Key findings / outcome

Balanced Random Forest led on cross-validation Macro F1 (0.4295) and achieved test Macro F1 0.4193 and accuracy 78.94%. Day of Week, Hour, and Number of Vehicles Involved were prominent grouped features.

Limitations

Fatal Injury recall was only 9.68% (3 of 31 cases), so the model is not suitable for autonomous safety decisions. External validation and better severe-class detection are needed.

Skills demonstrated

Multiclass classification, class-imbalance evaluation, hyperparameter tuning, cross-validation, statistical association testing, and responsible model interpretation.

09Deep Learning

MNIST Image Classification Using CNN

Developed and evaluated a convolutional neural network to classify handwritten digits using 60,000 MNIST training images and 10,000 test images. Applied image preprocessing, CNN training, validation, and confusion-matrix analysis, achieving 99.00% accuracy on the held-out test set.

Dataset

MNIST handwritten digits: 60,000 training and 10,000 test images; each image is 28 × 28 grayscale pixels across ten digit classes.

Approach

Normalized images, one-hot encoded labels, trained a CNN with 32- and 64-filter convolutional layers over five epochs, evaluated the held-out test set, inspected a confusion matrix, and saved the trained model.

Tools

Python, Jupyter Notebook, NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib, scikit-learn, and TensorFlow/Keras.

Key findings / outcome

Achieved 99.00% test accuracy (9,900 correct of 10,000 images) and 0.0309 test loss. Final training accuracy was 98.41% and validation accuracy was 98.97%; saved the model and evaluation outputs.

Limitations

MNIST performance may not generalize to noisy scans or unfamiliar handwriting. No robustness testing, per-class precision/recall reporting, or alternative-architecture comparison was included.

Skills demonstrated

Deep learning, CNN design, image preprocessing, model training and validation, confusion-matrix interpretation, and model export.
